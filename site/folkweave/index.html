A private timeline for your people.

Share posts and photographs with friends, family, or a small group. Catch up in order, reply to a post, and save something to come back to.

Current release: Folkweave 0.1.1

Self-hosted · Open source · Docker images, Linux binaries, Debian packages, and Gentoo packaging

Get started with FolkweaveExplore the source

What you can do today.

Invite your group

Each installation has its own accounts and invitations. Members can read earlier posts and photos. Owners manage invitations and membership, and can remove content.

Posts, photos, and replies

Write a short update or a longer post and attach up to four photographs. Add descriptions and arrange photos before posting. Replies appear with their post.

Catch up at your own pace

Posts stay in chronological order, with finite pages and a button for new arrivals. Editing or replying does not move an old post to the top. Saved posts are private.

Keep your contributions

Download your own posts, photographs, and replies. Verified automatic snapshots keep the database and shared photographs together; keep a protected copy on another machine too.

Current limits.

The current build has one group-wide audience. Parent-managed accounts and reviewed child feeds are planned work; parental approval controls are not implemented. Shared photos are resized and stripped of metadata. Original uploads are not retained, so keep your originals separately.

Backups and recovery.

The running service makes verified backups automatically each day. Keep a protected copy on another machine and practice restoring it. Read the backup and recovery guides.

Install Folkweave.

Use Docker Compose, a Linux archive or Debian package, or the Gentoo overlay. The deployment guide covers native services and configuration. You can also try a disposable local demo.

Build with Go 1.26 or newer and follow the disposable demo instructions. The demo runs on localhost with fictional sample content and its own temporary database. It removes that data when you stop it.

For your own installation, use the source setup guide and read the backup and export guidance. Keep the application behind HTTPS when sharing it online.

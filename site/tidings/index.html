Follow the sources you choose.

A self-hosted RSS and Atom reader for you and your people. Read a finite page, save something worthwhile, and put it down. Your subscriptions and reading state stay your own.

Current release: Tidings 0.4.0

Self-hosted · Open source · Docker images, Linux binaries, Debian packages, and Gentoo packaging

Get started with TidingsExplore the source

What you can do today.

Your own reading list

Follow RSS and Atom addresses, organize sources with tags, and create handpicked or tag-based feed groups. OPML import and export let you bring subscriptions with you.

An optional household table

Hosts can maintain shared sources and feed groups. Following the household table is off by default for everyone. Opt in or leave when you choose; personal subscriptions stay personal.

Reading state belongs to you

Read markers and saved stories are individual, even for shared sources. Opening a story does not mark it read automatically. Use the explicit action when you are finished.

A comfortable reading table

Roomier story cards, readable article formatting, and optional pictures supplied by feeds. Get full story brings an excerpt’s original article here. Hosts can enable local image caching, including inline illustrations.

Your keys, your choice

Keyboard shortcuts are off by default. Enable them in your account, or keep using Tridactyl and your browser’s own keys. Known tracking fields are removed from article links.

Know when fresh stories arrive.

Following a source starts its first fetch immediately. A live indicator shows progress, completed checks, and the next automatic check. Checks adapt to how often each source publishes, with more room between quiet or failing sources. Keep reading, then open the updated table when you are ready.

Suggestions chosen by your host.

Owners can enable categories such as news, science, culture, food, sports, nature, AI, technology, and security. Suggestions start disabled and never subscribe anyone automatically. You can always bring your own feeds.

Owners can also fetch all active feeds immediately from their account settings.

Backups and recovery.

The running service makes verified backups automatically each day. Keep a protected copy on another machine and practice restoring it. Read the backup and recovery guides.

Install Tidings.

Use Docker Compose, a Linux archive or Debian package, or the Gentoo overlay. The deployment guide covers native services and configuration. You can also try a disposable local demo.

Build with Go 1.26 or newer and follow the disposable demo instructions. The demo runs on localhost with fictional sample content and its own temporary database. It removes that data when you stop it.

For your own installation, use the source setup guide and read the backup and export guidance. Keep the application behind HTTPS when sharing it online.

Inspired by Miniflux.

Miniflux inspires the reading features. Tidings is built around SQLite, simple hosting, and households or small communities, with personal reading state and shared sources people choose to follow.
